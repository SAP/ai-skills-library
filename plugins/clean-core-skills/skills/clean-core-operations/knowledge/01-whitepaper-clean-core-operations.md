## Preface: Autonomous Enterprise and Clean Core

The **Autonomous Enterprise** is a business that can sense change, recommend actions, automate routine decisions, and continuously improve outcomes — while keeping people in control of strategy, governance, and exceptions.

**RISE with SAP Methodology** provides a guided path to the Autonomous Enterprise, built on:
- A standardized framework (SAP Activate)
- Expert guidance from SAP and partners
- An **agent-led toolchain** that simplifies migration, modernization, and continuous innovation

Clean core principles are essential in this context: by reducing unnecessary complexity, standardizing non-differentiating processes, governing extensions in an upgrade-stable manner, and improving data and process quality, customers create a foundation for faster innovation and lower operational risk.

## Chapter 1: Introduction

> *"Organizations which design systems […] are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations."* — Conway's Law

The systems your organization builds will always reflect its structures, governance, and communication patterns. This truth does not diminish at the conclusion of an implementation program — it compounds across every cycle.

**Clean core operations** is, by design, a **cross-principle discipline**. It encompasses all five clean core principles, embedding the governance, processes, toolchain, and software stack required for supporting both the transformation and subsequent continuous innovation cycles. It is not the fifth principle in a sequence — it is the principle that makes the other four sustainable, and the foundation on which the Autonomous Enterprise is built.

### The Five Guiding Principles

| # | Principle | One-liner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business Processes | Keep competitiveness while reducing complexity |
| 2 | Extensibility | Decouple extensions from the standard core |
| 3 | Data | Control data according to the latest standards |
| 4 | Integration | Keep the system landscape reliable and flexible |
| 5 | Operations | Keep the operations effective and efficient |

### The Four Cornerstones

| Cornerstone | Strategic role |
|---|---|
| **Governance foundation** | Ensures every architectural decision is traceable, justified, and connected to business outcomes |
| **Agile and secure engineering** | Keeps the clean core clean continuously, not just at go-live |
| **Agent-led toolchain** | Transforms operational data into executive insight and AI-assisted improvement guidance |
| **SAP innovation adoption** | Converts SAP's ongoing innovation into competitive advantage, absorbed safely and sustainably |

### The Strategic Payoff

- IT evolves from a cost center to an engine of the Autonomous Enterprise
- Governance becomes trusted, transparent, and measurable at every level
- Operational data creates a clear progression: transparency → understanding → intelligent recommendations
- Every clean core decision compounds value across every subsequent transformation cycle

## Chapter 2: Why Operations in the Context of Clean Core?

Conway's Law is why clean core operations is designed to connect all principles through governance, operational practices, an AI-enabled toolchain, and a software stack. It is not simply one standalone principle; it is the connective tissue that binds business processes, extensibility, data, and integration into a coherent, governed whole.

### The Relationship Between the Principles

**Business Processes** — the leading entity that governs design, execution, and continuous improvement of the business and architectural blueprint. Operations provides the framework to develop, manage, and monitor these processes.

**Extensibility** — Operations provides the core building blocks to manage execution, deployment, testing, and governance of extensions.

**Data** — Operations provides the vehicle and sets up the tooling to track data quality and compliance content.

**Integration** — Operations supports the establishment of governance and tracks the build, test, deployment, and ongoing monitoring of integrations.

**Operations** — a cross-dimension approach integrating best practices across governance, people, and processes, supported by an end-to-end toolchain. Without operational discipline, even the most carefully designed process landscape will deteriorate.

> *"Operations is the enabling foundation, providing the governance model, delivery cadence, toolchain, and organizational structures that enable well-designed, consistent implementation, and reliable maintenance of business processes across every phase of the transformation lifecycle."*

### Conway's Law at Enterprise Scale

At enterprise scale, fragmented communication structures lead to:
- Complex architectures with disconnected entities
- Duplication of requirements
- Poor end-user experience

The solution is **shared platform and governance** with consistency in approach across all domains — this is the core of how clean core governance addresses cross-principle topics.

## Chapter 4: The Four Cornerstones in Practice

### Cornerstone 1: Establish the Governance Foundation and Operating Model

Governance is the most consequential and most frequently overlooked factor in clean core transformation. Without governance structures that sustain clean core decisions across the entire lifecycle, the architecture will drift — extensions accumulate, integrations proliferate without oversight, technical debt grows silently.

**Strong governance is not rigid governance.** The goal is not to prevent change, but to ensure change is made consciously, traceably, and in alignment with the organization's strategic objectives.

#### A Connected Governance System, Not a Collection of Frameworks

The key insight: the frameworks, centers of excellence, and methodologies that support clean core are **not standalone constructs** — they are subsets and connected entities of a single, overarching governance model.

**The governing hierarchy:**

```
RISE with SAP Methodology (overarching framework)
│
└── Customer Center of Expertise (Customer COE) — organizational anchor
    ├── Architecture Governance Board — enterprise architecture oversight
    ├── Solution Standardization Board (SSB) — primary decision forum
    ├── Operations Control Center — operational visibility layer
    └── Domain-specific COEs and communities of practice
```

**Solution Standardization Board (SSB)** — evaluates extension requests, integration requirements, and architectural deviations against documented criteria. Requires:
- Clear mandate with formal membership from business and IT leaders
- Tiered decision-making model (routine decisions handled locally, higher-risk escalated)
- Every decision documented with business justification, technical impact assessment, and architectural rationale

**Architecture Governance Board** — provides enterprise architecture oversight, ensures solutions align with target architecture and clean core principles. Interacts with Operations Control Center using operational insights (incidents, performance trends, compliance findings) to validate and improve architectural standards.

**Operations Control Center** — surfaces system health, compliance status, and operational KPIs, connecting governance to the day-to-day reality of running the system.

**Customer COE** — the organizational embodiment of clean core governance, ensuring standards are understood, adopted, and sustained across all teams, phases, and delivery cycles. Organized around five capability areas: Strategy, Architecture & Innovation, Organization & Governance, Processes & Tools, People & Skills.

#### The Role of Methodologies as Connected Subsets

- **SAP Activate** — provides the phase cadence (Discover → Run) within which governance decisions are made and reviewed
- **Clean core principles** — provide the guardrails within which decisions are evaluated
- **RISE with SAP Methodology** — the overarching transformation framework within which both operate

### Cornerstone 2: Accelerate Delivery Through Agile and Secure Engineering Practices

Governance establishes the foundation. Agile and secure engineering practices allow organizations to move confidently within those boundaries, delivering value at speed without compromising clean core.

**The relationship:** Governance does not slow agile delivery down — it is the structure that makes agile delivery trustworthy. Without governance, agile methods produce velocity without direction and rapidly accumulate technical debt. Without agile methods, governance produces bureaucracy that slows delivery without improving quality.

**In practice:** Aligning delivery cadence with SAP Activate phases — each Explore, Realize, and Deploy cycle as a recurring operational rhythm, not a one-time project milestone. Each cycle delivers incremental business value, absorbs new SAP capabilities, and improves the clean core baseline.

**Secure engineering** is an embedded discipline within every phase, not an end-of-delivery gate:
- ABAP Cloud development standards
- ABAP Test Cockpit checks
- Extensibility guidelines
- Mandatory quality control measures ensuring extensions are governed, integrations are compliant, and the platform is secure throughout every delivery cycle

### Cornerstone 3: Enable and Adopt the Agent-Led Toolchain

The agent-led toolchain is not a collection of individual SAP tools. It is a coherent system in which artifacts flow across phases with traceability, decisions are auditable, adoption is measurable, and value is visible.

When artifacts are connected through a common integration layer and anchored to a unified data model, they tell the story of the transformation:
- Requirements trace to design decisions
- Design decisions trace to extensions and integrations
- Extensions and integrations trace to operational KPIs
- KPIs trace back to the business outcomes that justified the investment

**Key toolchain components:**
- SAP Signavio — business process management and optimization
- SAP LeanIX — architecture visualization and assessment
- SAP Cloud ALM — digital backbone; project management, solution operation, requirements management
- SAP Build — low-code/pro-code/genAI app creation, process automation
- SAP Test Automation by Tricentis / SAP Enterprise Continuous Testing by Tricentis
- WalkMe / SAP Enable Now — enablement, training, adoption
- SAP Integration Suite (incl. Cloud Transport Management, CI/CD)
- SAP Business Transformation Center — data transformation insights
- SAP Joule for Consultants / Developers — AI-assisted project acceleration

### Cornerstone 4: Leverage SAP Innovations to Remain Competitive

SAP Cloud ERP Private and the Autonomous Enterprise are living platforms. SAP continuously invests in new capabilities, performance improvements, security enhancements, and AI-driven features. These innovations are only available to organizations that stay current.

**Innovation compliance:** System stack must not be older than 24 months. Compliance is capped at the delivery of the last FPS (FPS3) of the release + 24 months.

**Security compliance:** FPS or SPS must not be older than 24 months. SAP guarantees security notes only for SPS shipped within the last 24 months.

**The release cadence:**
1. Each SAP S/4HANA release is shipped with an initial shipment stack
2. Three Feature Pack Stacks (FPS) delivered ~6 months apart, each containing smaller features and new innovations
3. Support Package Stacks (SPS) delivered regularly until end of maintenance

Staying current requires the governance and agile delivery practices from Cornerstones 1 and 2. Organizations that have invested in the first three cornerstones find staying current achievable and progressively easier. Those that have not find each deferred update harder than the last.

#### Security as an Imperative for Innovation Adoption

Staying current provides more than functional improvements — each update closes known security vulnerabilities. Organizations that maintain a consistent update cadence find their security posture progressively strengthens and innovation adoption becomes safer and more predictable with each cycle.

**SAP's Secure Operations Map** is a foundational requirement for organizations serious about adopting innovations — providing the structured framework for assigning security domain responsibilities, defining minimum security standards, and proactively managing cybersecurity risk.

## Chapter 5: Connecting Operations to Organizational Strategy

### Turning Clean Core Operations into a Strategic Asset

Many organizations implement strong governance and agile practices, yet the value remains invisible to senior leadership. Clean core operations addresses this directly through a connected measurement framework.

### Linking Strategy to the Customer COE Operating Model

The Customer COE is the structural bridge between company strategy and SAP operational practice. The Customer COE strategy must be derived from, and traceable to, the broader company strategy:

- Where company strategy calls for operational excellence → define what this means in SAP terms: which processes must perform at what standard, which KPIs indicate that standard, which governance mechanisms ensure it is sustained
- Where company strategy calls for growth through innovation → define the innovation adoption cadence, clean core guardrails, and toolchain integrations

### The RISE with SAP Methodology Dashboard

The central visibility mechanism. More than a reporting tool — a comprehensive set of dashboards designed to track and monitor status, gather insights, and provide actionable recommendations at every stage of the SAP Activate lifecycle.

Operates across two layers:
1. **High-level overview** — executive summary connecting RISE with SAP Methodology status to clean core adherence and business outcome progress
2. **Topic-related deep dive dashboards** — operations, data, extensions, integrations; detailed insight for identifying improvement actions

Most valuable when integrated into the governance cadence established by the Customer COE, not consulted on an ad hoc basis.

### The Clean Core Operations KPI Framework

Six KPI categories, each addressing a distinct dimension of operational and transformation performance:

| KPI Category | What it measures | Primary audience |
|---|---|---|
| **Software stack** | System currency relative to SAP release cadence; FPS/SPS age vs. 24-month threshold | CIO/CTO |
| **Enable agility through agent-led toolchain adoption** | Degree to which the agent-led toolchain is adopted across the delivery lifecycle | CIO |
| **Adopt clean core requirements** | Embedded requirements validation; proportion of deviations; technical debt trend | CIO/SSB |
| **Continuously improve agile delivery and operational excellence** | Delivery velocity, quality, and operational stability | CIO/COO |
| **Enduring operational efficiency** | Stability, reliability, and responsiveness of SAP systems and business processes | COO |
| **Embed secure operations** | Compliance with security standards; SAP baseline security adoption | CISO |

> *Full per-KPI calculation detail: `knowledge/03-kpi-operations.md`*

### The Governance and Maturity Model — Eight Practices

The KPI framework connects directly to eight maturity practices, each scored 0–5:

| Practice | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **Maturity of organizational governance** | Governance structures, forums, program governance model; agile adaptation embedded in governance routines |
| **Maturity of integrated business transformation toolchain** | End-to-end toolchain effectiveness across architecture, process, requirements, change/deployment, testing, training, monitoring |
| **Maturity of continuous improvement for an enduring clean core** | Systematic requirements management; adherence monitoring; revisiting deviations across successive innovation cycles |
| **Maturity of test management and execution** | Testing strategies, coverage, automation, tool usage, integration with requirements management |
| **Maturity of software delivery efficiency** | Speed of software delivery while maintaining quality and reliability |
| **Maturity of secure operations** | Security strategy alignment, hardening, monitoring, security integrated into solution design |
| **Maturity of release management** | Release planning, architecture alignment, multi-component coordination, risk mitigation, continuous improvement |
| **Maturity of IT operations excellence** | Monitoring, alerting, event management, reporting, operational transition, incident analysis, ongoing optimization |

The eight practices form an **interconnected measurement system** — maturity of each dimension influences the achievable maturity of others. The framework is designed to identify the constraints limiting progress across the system as a whole.

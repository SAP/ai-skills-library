## Operations Overview — 8 Practices (Slides 82-83)

The Operations principle has **8 practices** organized across two slides:

**Practices Overview I (Slide 82):**

| Practice | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **OPS-01: Clean Core Governance** | Ensure consistent approach, establish organizational governance, achieve new operating model, manage implementations and innovation | Enable the organization for continuous innovation adoption |
| **OPS-02: Business Transformation Toolchain** | Increase speed and agility, enhance transparency and collaboration, streamline processes with integrated toolchain, accelerate innovation and value realization | Increase speed, agility, transparency and collaboration between business and IT functions |
| **OPS-03: Continuous Improvement for an Enduring Clean Core** | Monitor ongoing technical debt, necessitate continuous revisiting of requirements to reduce technical debt, improve Clean Core adoption in future cycles | Business requirements may necessitate accruing technical debt; requirements should be continuously revisited |
| **OPS-04: Test Management and Execution** | Early agile-based testing approaches, test automation increases quality, reduces timelines from requirements to deploy | Testing is critical in transformation |

**Practices Overview II (Slide 83):**

| Practice | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **OPS-05: Software Delivery Efficiency** | Enhance business agility, increase speed of software delivery, maintain quality and reliability, ensure optimal business value realization | Delivery of software must increase in speed but ensure quality and reliability |
| **OPS-06: Secure Operations** | Keep S/4HANA SPS/FPS releases up to date, embed secure operations early in design, align release plan with Enterprise and transitional architecture | Secure operations are crucial for business success; reduce system intrusion risk, ensure data confidentiality |
| **OPS-07: Release Management** | Align release plan with Enterprise and transitional architecture, sync releases with business critical events, manage risk with rollback, monitoring, and continuous improvement strategies | Effective release management is essential for a stable, efficient, and agile SAP environment |
| **OPS-08: IT Operations Excellence** | Implement comprehensive monitoring and alerting across all levels, utilize event management approach with appropriate tools, establish management reporting and dashboards, develop a transition to operations strategy, monitor and analyze incidents vs. alerts, continuously enhance technical efficiency | IT Operations Excellence ensures system reliability, performance, security, and scalability |

## OPS-01: Clean Core Governance

**Why:** In order to ensure consistency in approach and enable the organization for continuous innovation adoption, the operations principle recommends to establish the organizational governance and practices to achieve the new operating model and effectively manage implementations and ongoing innovation cycles.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Have the required roles, responsibilities, and tasks been established within the customer Centre of Expertise as appropriate for the project phase? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Has a clear program setup been established for the Solution Standardization Board, including the required parties (implementation partner, customer steering, SAP as appropriate) with Chair, Architects, requestors, SMEs, etc., and defined processes and procedures? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the methodology prescribed by SAP Activate or similar being followed, including utilizing provided accelerators and agile methodologies? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the RISE with SAP methodology being followed, including engagement with the RISE Expert teams, clean core Quality Gates, and clean core success plan? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an established collaboration model between business and IT covering operational, tactical, and strategic level topics? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| For major transformations, is there a well-aligned and structured program governance model including customer stakeholders, SAP and SI in accordance with the SAP Activate Guidelines? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Governance is missing or ad hoc | Governance is informal or missing; roles and methodologies are unclear or unused |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Some structure exists, inconsistently applied | Some roles and processes are defined, but governance and methodology use are inconsistent |
| **3 — Proficient** | Key governance structures in place and used | Key roles and boards are in place; SAP methodologies are followed with moderate consistency |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrated, well-governed, and collaborative | Well-structured governance with strong methodology adoption and integrated business–IT collaboration |
| **5 — Expert** | Strategic, agile, and continuously optimized governance | Governance is fully institutionalized, agile, and strategic, with seamless business–IT integration and continuous improvement |

## OPS-02: Business Transformation Toolchain

**Why:** To increase speed, agility, transparency and collaboration between business and IT functions, streamlined implementation and operations processes are supported by an integrated toolchain to accelerate innovation and value realization.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is an established tool utilized to manage business Solution Strategy and Enterprise Architecture with strong integration to processes, in accordance with the Architecture Practice? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the design of Processes managed and governed, with collaboration between IT and Business conducted in such a way that process models are reusable and integrated with related tools for architecture and application lifecycle management? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a tool-based approach to managing the requirements to deploy the process? Is this tool technically integrated with process management and monitoring tools? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an established functional test automation tool that is integrated with the solution documentation and business/IT requirements? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there tooling in place to roll out and manage/monitor digital training and enablement, which is linked to the processes being implemented and continuously updated? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are there tools in place to support monitoring of the solution from a technical, application, user, and process perspective? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Tools are absent or uncoordinated | Minimal or no toolchain; manual processes dominate |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Basic tools exist, but siloed | Tools exist but are siloed and not well-integrated |
| **3 — Proficient** | Tools moderately integrated, supporting collaboration | Key tools are in place with some integration and collaboration support |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrated toolchain with automation and governance | Toolchain is integrated and supports end-to-end collaboration and automation |
| **5 — Expert** | Strategic, optimized toolchain enabling innovation | Fully integrated, strategic toolchain driving continuous innovation and business value |

## OPS-03: Continuous Improvement for an Enduring Clean Core

**Why:** As business requirements may necessitate accruing technical debt based on capability availability and roadmap considerations, requirements should be continuously revisited in future innovation cycles to continuously improve Clean Core adoption.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there an established requirements management process agreed upon between the business and implementation team that embeds a fit-to-standard methodology with a strong focus on business value to approve deviations? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an established approach that documents and tracks decisions on requirements made within the Solution Standardization Board or similar, where deviations to standard are approved? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the continuous adherence to the clean core methodology tracked, and are all stakeholders onboarded to that approach (customer, implementation partner, AMS provider, SAP, etc.)? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a proactive approach to look for new opportunities to revert to standard when innovating in an existing process? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are processes, data, integrations, and extensions continuously monitored to identify opportunities to reduce complexity or ensure adoption? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | No Clean Core practices in place | No formal Clean Core governance; deviations are unmanaged |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Basic processes, inconsistent application | Basic requirement processes exist; Clean Core awareness is low |
| **3 — Proficient** | Structured Clean Core tracking and governance | Structured governance and monitoring of Clean Core; deviations are tracked |
| **4 — Advanced** | Continuous improvement and deviation reviews in place | Strong governance with continuous improvement and Clean Core adherence |
| **5 — Expert** | Fully institutionalized Clean Core approach | Clean Core is embedded in culture and continuously optimized across the lifecycle |

## OPS-04: Test Management and Execution

**Why:** Testing is a critical component of any transformation, and ensuring early agile based testing approaches and test automation is key to increased quality with reducing timelines from requirements to deploy.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there an established test strategy covering objectives, scope, test types, timeline, entry/exit criteria, test environments, defect management, test organization, communication and reporting, measurements, metrics, and tools? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is test coverage measured and are there available unit (developer) tests for all developed code, integrated into CI/CD pipelines or change management tools where relevant? (e.g., ATC, ABAP Unit, Jest, etc.) | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are documented requirements technically linked to functional tests and processes? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is testing managed with an enterprise-ready tool? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an investment in end-to-end functional tests linked to business processes, and API testing? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an investment in functional test automation as part of project delivery to enable continuous testing and reduce regression effort within waves and agile releases and also in readiness for future innovations? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Manual, informal testing | Testing is manual, unstructured, and poorly documented |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Some structure and tools | Basic strategy exists; testing is partially documented and manual |
| **3 — Proficient** | Structured, tool-supported testing with traceability | Formal strategy in place, tools used, and testing linked to requirements |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrated testing with automation and CI/CD | Automation and CI/CD support agile testing and business process alignment |
| **5 — Expert** | End-to-end continuous testing with traceability | Fully automated, agile-aligned testing with traceability, coverage, and performance monitoring |

## OPS-05: Software Delivery Efficiency

**Why:** In order to achieve business agility, the delivery of software to support business needs must increase in speed of delivery but ensure quality and reliability to ensure overall business value realization.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there an effort to improve/increase the velocity of changes moving through the landscape? Velocity includes optimizing the change frequency, volume, execution of tests, and speed of deployment (from initial requirement to realization). | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the quality of software delivery tracked through measuring the test automation rate, test coverage, automated code quality checks, and security and compliance issues? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the efficiency of software deployment and release tracked? This includes the innovation rate, deployment frequency, release frequency, lead time to production, and mean time to recover. | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the reliability of software delivery tracked? This includes tracking ongoing user incident volumes, alert and event volumes, application and process performance, availability, and data consistency. | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Manual delivery, no KPIs | Software delivery is manual, slow, and unmeasured. No tracking of quality, velocity, or reliability |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Basic tracking and improvements in place | Some delivery metrics are tracked and efforts to improve speed or quality exist, but processes are still siloed and partly manual |
| **3 — Proficient** | Metrics and automation improving quality/speed | Key delivery KPIs (velocity, test automation, code quality) are tracked and inform improvement. Basic CI/CD and automation are in use |
| **4 — Advanced** | CI/CD integrated with delivery and reliability | Software delivery is agile, metrics-driven, and automated across pipelines. Performance, reliability, and recovery metrics are actively monitored |
| **5 — Expert** | Fully agile, optimized delivery pipeline | End-to-end delivery is fully automated and agile, with optimized velocity, quality, and reliability. Continuous improvement is institutionalized |

## OPS-06: Secure Operations

**Why:** Secure Operations within the overall Enterprise landscape is critical to the success of your business in order to reduce risk of system intrusions, ensure confidentiality of business data, ensure authenticity of users and reduce risk of business outages due to wrong user interactions.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a security strategy created in alignment with the SAP Secure Operations Map reference models? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a process dedicated to security hardening and patch management? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the system monitored in accordance with the SAP security baseline, and has it been adapted to the specific business needs? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are secure operations embedded early within the design and realization of a solution? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Reactive or missing security practices | No formal security strategy; minimal patching or monitoring; secure operations not considered in project phases |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Basic security measures in place | A security baseline exists; some patching and monitoring processes are followed, but implementation is inconsistent and reactive |
| **3 — Proficient** | Structured security strategy with aligned practices | A defined security strategy exists, aligned with reference models. Hardening, monitoring, and patching are structured and integrated across systems |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrated and proactive secure operations | Security is proactively embedded in design, realization, and operations. Monitoring is tailored to business needs and threat models |
| **5 — Expert** | Embedded, adaptive, and business-aligned security | Security is a continuous, adaptive process embedded across the lifecycle, with real-time monitoring, early design integration, and full business alignment |

## OPS-07: Release Management

**Why:** Effective release management is essential for maintaining a stable, efficient, and agile SAP environment. It ensures that enhancements, patches, and upgrades are delivered in a controlled, predictable manner, minimizing risk and disruption. Additionally, it ensures that you stay on the latest SAP release, enabling the adoption of SAP's latest innovations.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a plan in place to keep the core S/4HANA SPS/FPS on a release no older than 24 months? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a release plan aligned with the overall Enterprise Architecture and transitional architecture? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are releases aligned with the business needs (e.g., for major releases or upgrades with critical outages)? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Are releases planned across software components (e.g., SaaS, PaaS, and IaaS products), including alignment with vendor release strategies? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an approach in place to manage risk, implement rollback strategies, monitor releases, and help ensure continuous improvement? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Ad hoc, reactive releases | Releases are unplanned, often delayed, and reactive to urgent needs. There is no formal release calendar, upgrade planning, or risk mitigation |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Basic release planning | A release schedule exists but is not consistently aligned with business or architecture strategy. Risk and rollback strategies are limited |
| **3 — Proficient** | Structured release cycles and alignment | Releases follow a defined plan aligned to enterprise architecture and business needs. Risk mitigation and monitoring practices are in place |
| **4 — Advanced** | Coordinated, risk-managed releases | Releases are coordinated across all relevant SAP components (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS). Vendor timelines are considered, and robust rollback, testing, and monitoring are standard |
| **5 — Expert** | Agile, enterprise-wide release strategy | Release management is strategic and agile — fully integrated with architecture, business cycles, and vendor roadmaps. Includes proactive risk management, innovation adoption, and continuous improvement |

## OPS-08: IT Operations Excellence

**Why:** IT Operations Excellence is crucial for SAP transformations because it ensures system reliability, performance, security, and scalability, enabling seamless business processes and minimizing disruptions.

### Evaluation Criteria

| Evaluation Question | Current Score | Target Score |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a consideration of requirements for monitoring and alerting for all implementations and developments at both technical, application, and process levels? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there an operation according to an event management approach (e.g., ITIL)? Are there tools in place to support this approach? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there management reporting and dashboarding established, with defined roles and responsibilities? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a transition to operations approach established where the impact of a transformation on the IT support organization is evaluated, along with the implementation of tools, processes, and organizational requirements to support post-GoLive operations? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is the overall volume of incidents vs. alerts monitored, with efforts to identify areas for improvement through building requirements for the development pipeline to enhance the business suite? | 0–5 | 0–5 |
| Is there a continuous effort to optimize the technical efficiency and performance of the landscape? | 0–5 | 0–5 |

### Maturity Rating Guidance

| Rating | Short Description | Long Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Not Started** | Not started | Not started |
| **1 — Beginner** | Reactive, fragmented operations | Operations are primarily reactive. Monitoring is limited, and there's no structured handover from projects to operations. Minimal reporting or tooling |
| **2 — Intermediate** | Some structure and tools in place | Event management is introduced, and monitoring covers some layers (mostly technical). Reporting exists but is limited in scope and ownership |
| **3 — Proficient** | Structured operations with monitoring and reporting | Monitoring is implemented across technical, application, and process levels. Tools support operations, and dashboards/reporting are established with clear roles |
| **4 — Advanced** | Integrated operations with continuous improvement | IT operations are proactive, using ITIL-based frameworks. Transformation-to-operations handovers are formalized. Metrics and alerts are tied to dev pipelines |
| **5 — Expert** | Predictive, value-focused operations | Operations are predictive and fully integrated with development and business outcomes. Performance and incident data drive continual optimization across the landscape |

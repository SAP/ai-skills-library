## Strategic Context: Autonomous Enterprise and Clean Core

The clean core strategy helps organizations establish the standard, adaptable ERP foundation they need for continuous transformation and for adopting AI-enabled capabilities on the path to the **Autonomous Enterprise** — a business that can sense change, recommend actions, automate routine decisions, and continuously improve outcomes while keeping people in control.

**Clean core is not about limiting scope.** A common misconception is that a clean core means limiting what is deployed and moving more capabilities outside the core via point solutions. This underutilizes the integrated digital enterprise core. Clean core means using standard capabilities wherever they provide the required business outcome, and extending only where differentiation creates clear value — in a governed, upgrade-stable way that avoids new technical debt.

**Why business processes are the first principle:** Business processes have priority over the other four clean core dimensions because processes define what extensions, integrations, data, and operational requirements are needed. Standardizing non-differentiating processes reduces extensions, integrations, and data conversions downstream, and creates the process consistency required for reliable automation and agentic AI.

**Connection to the Autonomous Enterprise:** Business process standardization provides the transparency and governance needed to scale automation and agentic AI responsibly. Standardized processes form the reliable backbone on which new SAP Autonomous Suite capabilities (Autonomous Finance, Autonomous Spend, Autonomous SCM, Autonomous HCM, Autonomous CX) can run.

## The Business Processes Principle — At a Glance

> *"Business processes maintain competitiveness while reducing complexity."*

The business processes dimension of the clean core strategy increases value potential by:
- Focusing on end-to-end harmonization
- Reducing unnecessary extensions, integrations, and data conversions
- Creating the process consistency required for reliable automation and agentic AI
- Aligning solutions with strategic objectives from the start of the transformation

**Three main aspects of the principle (Figure 3 in the whitepaper):**

1. **Organizational structure, technical foundation, and transformation methodology** — including roles and responsibilities, end-to-end toolchain, and process governance

2. **Prioritized and segmented business process hierarchy** — including end-to-end and functional process views

3. **Align to-be process design and technical realization** — by leveraging best practices where possible, and centrally governing the to-be process, business requirements, and design approval

## Business Process Management (BPM)

**Definition:** Business process management is a practice or discipline that aims to improve an organization's process effectiveness and sustainability. It entails effective modeling, automation, optimization, and analysis of end-to-end business processes.

In SAP transformations — whether implementing SAP Cloud ERP Private, modernizing an existing landscape, or driving continuous improvement — BPM plays a critical role linking business process changes, IT architecture, and innovations to measurable business benefits. It also provides the process transparency and governance needed to scale automation and agentic AI responsibly.

**A good BPM practice delivers:**

| Outcome | Description |
|---|---|
| **Standardization** | Commodity end-to-end processes as per industry best practices and software vendor recommendations |
| **Reuse** | Standardized processes across different business verticals |
| **A case for change** | Process improvements and automation through analysis and benchmarking of process effectiveness |
| **Identification** | Of the differentiating processes required by the organization for maintaining competitiveness |
| **Process governance** | Framework to drive differentiating value and standardization; bridges architecture and solution governance to achieve the evolution of intended business capabilities |

SAP recommends establishing BPM as an **ongoing organizational practice** — not a one-time pre-transformation activity — that supports current transformations and future innovation initiatives.

## Fit to Standard in SAP Cloud ERP Private

**Fit to standard** is a fundamental new way of thinking in the cloud era. It provides the ability to rapidly adopt standard best practices and leverage cloud technology to establish a foundation for engaging in continuous innovation.

Standardizing commodity processes creates real business value by:
- Reducing complexity and lowering maintenance effort
- Freeing resources for differentiation
- Maximizing efficiency for industry-standard commodity processes (which are market-leading and optimized by definition)
- Enabling higher agility, flexibility, and collaboration

A well-defined fit to standard approach enables an organization to optimally use business and technology innovations that the cloud offers.

### Elements of the Fit to Standard Approach

Any cloud transformation relies on three elements:

1. **Choice of a standard software solution** for adoption
2. **Implementation content — Business Process content** (SAP Best Practices and SAP standard content)
3. **Implementation content — Technical solution accelerators**

### Fit to Standard Process — Before Starting

Before starting fit to standard sessions for each process area, a **Key Decision Document (KDD)** must be in place. This baselines the inventory of standard processes against which fit to standard sessions are conducted. Additional software solutions may be decided at a later stage, subject to forthcoming delta requirements. KDDs are managed within the Solution Standardization Board (SSB) as part of SAP Activate.

### Standard Process Archetypes

Standard processes have five archetypes:

| Archetype | Description |
|---|---|
| **Best practices** | Proven methods and techniques delivering predictable outcomes |
| **Industry standards** | Acceptable practices within a particular industry sector |
| **Regulatory standards** | Legal/reporting requirements in specific regulatory regimes |
| **Internal standards** | Specific organizational processes for unique objectives and differentiating capabilities |
| **International standards** | Standards developed by organizations like ISO that an organization may subscribe to |

**Industries covered:** Consumer industry (consumer products, core retail, fashion & vertical business), Discrete manufacturing (industrial machinery & components, automotive), Energy & natural resources (chemicals, mill products, mining, oil & gas, integrated utilities), Service industry (telecommunications, airline back offices, professional services), Industry supporting (connected assets, supply chain executions, research & development, subscription billing and revenue management)

- **SAP Reference Solution Architecture:** Explores how SAP addresses a customer's business challenge with its unique product portfolio.

Content accessible via **SAP Signavio Process Explorer**.

- Content should be aligned to latest SAP product capabilities and reference architecture for SAP Cloud ERP Private

- No custom developments / WRICEFs, extensions in the ecosystem, or customer existing processes for the proposed fit to standard walkthrough
- Content must have business process flows, configured system extended on SAP Best Practices, system walkthrough script, and configuration documentation

## Delta Requirements

**Objective:** Obtain information on deviations from vendor-specific standard processes and systems. Delta requirements are triaged into fit and gap items, with detailed classifications used in later phases to define and package work.

### Capture Levels

1. **Process and solution delta requirements** — at individual process-step level
2. **Data object delta requirements** — at individual data-object level, linked to the requiring process

### Each Delta Requirement Captures WHO, WHAT, WHY

- **WHO:** The actual impacted user role or job role
- **WHAT:** Description of the requirement, including the need or missing details
- **WHY:** Why the requirement is important; expected results, regulations, legal compliance, and outcomes

## Fit/Gap Classification

SAP's recommended fit/gap classification for SAP Cloud ERP Private transformations is driven by the "5 Golden Rules for Deploying SAP S/4HANA with a Cloud Mindset" and the Solution Standardization Board (SSB). Classification is aligned with clean core principles across process, extensibility, integration, and data dimensions.

| Type | Code | Classification | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fit** | F-SC | SAP Best Practice / SAP standard content | SAP content (SAP Best Practices, EML, or SAP standard content) is identified as a fit |
| **Fit** | F-VS | Software vendor SaaS applications | Software vendor applications as a fit to introduce solution capabilities outside of core (e.g. industry cloud solutions, SAP Ariba, SAP BTP applications, third-party applications) |
| **Fit** | F-EC | Extended configuration aligned to SAP standard | Additional settings or changes to existing settings compared to delivered SAP content (e.g. organizational structures, company codes); also latest innovations as SAP software capability (e.g. embedded AI, latest software module in SAP Cloud ERP Private) |
| **Gap** | G-EC | Extended configuration not aligned to SAP standard | Approach/solution to meet the business need results in complex system maintenance and/or lack of system optimization, leading to non-conformance to fit to standard (e.g. using capabilities in SAP Cloud ERP Private compatibility packs, or creating extensive document types needing maintenance and security control) |
| **Gap** | G-RP | Road map path to standard | A temporary gap where a potential SAP road map item will fulfill the requirement and resolve the gap in the future |
| **Gap** | G-EP | Extensions: Process deviation | Requirement to extend in presentation, application, or data tier to meet specific process deviation from standard system behavior. These extensions lead to non-conformance to fit to standard and require business justification |
| **Gap** | G-EN | Extensions: Non-process | Requirement to extend in presentation, application, or data tier to meet a specific project or business need (e.g. a conversion program, a business report not triggering execution, an output email or form) |
| **Gap** | G-ET | Extensions: Temporary | Requirement temporary in nature, to be purged at a later point in the transformation journey (e.g. an interim solution between release cycles, temporary resolution to a road map item) |
| **Gap** | G-AN | Machine Learning, RPA, Custom innovation | Requirement to either consume custom AI capabilities or build a solution/application as part of an innovation project |

### Gap Management

Practice to identify and document potential common gaps across industry peers, for alignment with the product vendor on the future road map path via the [SAP Customer Influence Portal](https://influence.sap.com).

### Delta Design

Solution design required for extended configuration and gap extensions, including completing enterprise structure and master data design.

## Solution Standardization Board (SSB)

The SSB is the governance body for clean core compliance across a transformation program.

**Role:** Oversee compliance with the 5 Golden Rules and clean core principles. Responsible for approving all extensions, integrations, data activities, and the adoption of processes that deviate from the rules and philosophy.

**Setup:** Established within the customer IT organization, reporting to the project sponsor and steering committee. SAP recommends setting up the SSB at the end of the Discover phase or early in the Prepare phase of SAP Activate.

**Scope of SSB governance:**
- Process deviations that impact program scope and design principles
- In-app, side-by-side, and developer extensibility (especially extensions deviating from [Extensibility Guidance for SAP Cloud ERP Private](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_CLOUD/6aa39f88fe7c4c0e93c47e3efab35d2f/5b9e49f9e440458c92044d0fa0a10d00.html))
- Required integrations with emphasis on use of allow-listed APIs and compliance with Golden Rules
- Architectural, cross-team design, and data decisions that deviate from clean core principles

**Monitoring:** The SSB tracks progress toward the objectives in the clean core success plan using the **clean core runbook**, set up in SAP Cloud ALM.

### Three Pillars of SSB Governance

**Architecture governance** — Capabilities & Architecture
- Which capability and which application is impacted by the transformation?
- In which backlog does the change go and how important is it, based on business impact?
- Can the requirement be covered by solutions in target state reference architecture, before investing into a new tool? ("Reuse, buy, build" principle)

**Process governance** — Process Analysis & Process Design
- How to define and implement a standard process across business units, divisions, and geographies using SAP Best Practices and standard content?
- How to keep deviations like localization requirements under control? ("Keep it simple")

**Solution governance** — Solution Design
- Do I need to customize my ERP core code or can I cover my requirements in a smarter way?
- Is there unused standard functionality or the possibility to use SAP BTP for extension? ("Keep the core clean")
- How can I keep the effort for performing changes and the negative change management impact on business as low as possible? ("Minimize the effort for change")

**Calibrated by:** SAP Reference Business Architecture, key design decisions, fit to standard, gap management.

### When is a Process Deviation Justified?

A process deviation should be considered only when **at least one** of these criteria is met:
1. Industry compliance requirements
2. Local regulatory requirements
3. Market-differentiating capability — bringing quantifiable value to the core business

Alternatives to deviations should always be evaluated first, including usability improvements, process automation, reporting and insights, and new innovations such as those built on SAP Business AI Platform.

## Getting Started with Fit to Standard

Four recommended steps to get started:

1. **Start with** fit to standard approach and create champions on the methodology and tooling within the organization. Drive the fit to standard mindset in business and IT stakeholders at all levels. Refer to SAP Activate methodology for RISE with SAP for details.

2. **List** the global end-to-end processes for your organization, assign global business process owners and IT solution owners, and ensure core business and IT teams are trained on the latest SAP Cloud ERP Private capabilities and SAP standard process and system content before starting fit to standard preparation activities.

3. **Identify** value creation opportunities and business value drivers leveraging process standardization, latest solution capabilities, and innovation adoption. For system conversion and selective data transition scenarios, analyze current processes to identify opportunities to move to standard and optimize.

4. **Establish** the Solution Standardization Board and its design principles based on identified business value drivers and clean core principles. Implement an agent-led toolchain for the transformation, with measurable clean core baselines (KPIs and thresholds) and periodic monitoring. Communicate goals, design principles, and guidelines to all owners and teams, focusing on standardizing commodity business capabilities and allowing business-value-driven deviations only for market-differentiating capabilities.
